Further North · a story

Bet on Europe, until round eight

Each round of the game shows two cities, each with its country, and nothing else. If you don’t know where the cities are, is there a rule you could play off the card?

I re-drew the game’s ten pairs for each of its first days, to : pairs among cities. A day’s ten is fixed by its date, so the days still to come are already decided. For three rules, each red dot is how often the city the rule picks was the further north, counting only the pairs where it picks one.

The grey is the test: the same rule played on a random label, times. Picking the European city is right of the time, well clear of the grey. Picking the capital: , a coin flip. Picking the bigger city: . It loses.

A likely reason, which these data do not test: every European city in the game is north of 35°, so it wins whenever the gap is wider than a country. When the gap is 50 km, being in the far north tells you nothing.

Chapter 2 · The rule that loses

Size is not the clue

Over the year, the bigger of the two cities was the further north only of the time; none of random shuffles of the populations did as badly. So bet on the smaller one?

Split the pairs. When the two cities are on different continents, the bigger one wins . When they are on the same continent it wins , and of the random shuffles do as badly: inside the grey.

So size was never the clue; the continent was. Of the cross-continent pairs the bigger city lost, were a big Asian city against a smaller European one: Europe’s rule again, wearing a disguise.

Epilogue · What to tap

Early on, Europe. Late, you have to know

In the first two rounds of Further North, when one of the cities is in Europe, tap it: it will be wrong about one time in twenty. Being the capital tells you nothing, and being the bigger city is only the continent again.

By the last three rounds, nothing on the card helps. That is the game working: those rounds are won by knowing where the cities are, which is the only thing it was meant to test.
